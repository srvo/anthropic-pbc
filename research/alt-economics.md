# Alternative models and the people the argument leaves out

Two indexes for open lines L2 ("What would we do if we really cared about the environment, the world and flourishing?") and L3 (harm borne for possible gain). `alt-economics.tsv` holds 22 models, each shown at its strongest with its strongest criticism. `unaddressed.tsv` holds 20 groups that bear effects of frontier AI, checked against `argument/parties.tsv`. Retrieved 2026-11-05; no row is a card until a quote is pinned.

## How the models cluster

1. **Limits first.** Permacomputing, doughnut economics, degrowth and post-growth, circular economy, regenerative economics. They start from what the Earth can bear. Only the doughnut gives a measurable ceiling and floor; only degrowth says throughput must fall; circular economy sets no ceiling.
2. **Who governs.** Ostrom's commons, commons-based peer production, cooperatives, platform cooperatives, data cooperatives, social and solidarity economy, exit to community. Control goes to members or users, who are not everyone affected; limits are mostly absent.
3. **Purpose locks.** Steward ownership and purpose trusts, perpetual-purpose trusts, community land trusts, data trusts, benefit corporations and PBCs. These protect a purpose from investors. The PBC is the weakest: directors owe no duty to the people it names, and only 2% holders can sue (Del. Code tit. 8, §§ 365, 367). A purpose trust binds stewards, but not to the affected.
4. **Public provision.** Public utility and public-option compute (the Public AI Network, Switzerland's Apertus). Scale becomes a public decision, but is not limited by design.
5. **Relational and rights-based frames.** Buen Vivir and the rights of nature, Ubuntu, Indigenous data sovereignty (CARE), the wellbeing economy. These widen who counts: nature (with legal standing in Ecuador and New Zealand), descendants, peoples as collectives.

## Which answer L2 most directly

L2's draft answer has three parts (a resource budget; a seat and veto for those who bear the effects; compute fitted inside what is left). One model carries each:

- **Doughnut economics** supplies the budget: an ecological ceiling and a social floor. It lacks an allocation rule, the gap L2 already names (Bjørn et al.).
- **Ostrom's commons** supplies the seat and the veto. Those who depend on a resource make its rules, monitor it and sanction overuse. Its weakness is scale: the principles come from small, bounded communities.
- **Permacomputing** supplies "fit compute inside what is left." "Not doing" and design for intermittent renewable power make compute answer to available energy. It is a practice, not an institution.

Rights of nature (Buen Vivir) is the strongest supplement. It gives the ecosystems that supply compute standing in court.

## Who parties.tsv leaves out

Seven of the 20 groups already have a party row: future-people, low-use-countries, data-workers, authors, host-communities, workers and misuse-targets. Every one of them except authors is recorded with no standing. Thirteen have no row:

- children
- non-human life and ecosystems
- the 2.2 billion people offline
- speakers of non-English and low-resource languages
- dead authors and their estates
- communities near mines for chips and battery minerals
- electricity ratepayers
- people subject to automated decisions
- disabled people
- Indigenous peoples, as holders of data and knowledge
- older people
- incarcerated people
- migrants and people on the move

Most have an outside voice (UNICEF, a UN Special Rapporteur, a utility commission, a rights-of-nature court); none has a lever on Anthropic. Four rows rest on other firms' conduct (OpenAI's Kenyan labellers, xAI in Memphis, Finnish prisons, the Dutch benefits algorithm); their cards must say so. Effects are as the sources document them; these indexes do not speak for any group.
